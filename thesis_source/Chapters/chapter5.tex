This chapter reports the experimental results of the five 3D architectures on the ADNI cohort, under the strict subject-level five-fold protocol defined in Chapter~\ref{cha:MethodologyAndImplementationU}. Results are presented for the headline binary CN-vs-AD task and the harder three-class CN/MCI/AD benchmark, followed by an analysis of the single-vs-hybrid question, the effect of medical transfer learning, the behaviour of the transformer models, and an explainability study. All numbers are mean $\pm$ standard deviation across the five subject-disjoint folds, with macro-F1 as the primary metric.

\section{Experimental Setup Recap}
Every model was trained and evaluated on the \emph{same} folds (\texttt{seed = 42}), with the same preprocessing, classifier head, and training recipe; only the backbone differs. Predictions are aggregated to the subject level by averaging a subject's per-scan logits. Because CN is the majority class and MCI the minority, accuracy alone is misleading, so macro-F1 (the unweighted mean of per-class F1) is treated as the headline number and drives model selection and early stopping.

\section{Does the Hybrid Actually Help? (RQ1)}
The research question was whether fusing local (CNN) and global (transformer) 3D features beats either alone. The evidence is nuanced and reported honestly:
\begin{itemize}
    \item On the binary task, the hybrid CNN--ViT is the \emph{best} model, but its $+1.2$ accuracy / $+1.4$ macro-F1 margin over the standalone DenseNet is \emph{within one standard deviation}. The two are statistically tied; the hybrid gives a small, consistent edge rather than a dramatic jump.
    \item The gain depends entirely on \emph{which} branches are fused. CNN$+$ViT (a strong CNN plus a light transformer) helps; Swin$+$ViT (two transformers) is much weaker, confirming that the CNN branch carries most of the signal on this data scale.
\end{itemize}
The honest conclusion is that on a small 3D dataset a well-trained CNN is a very strong baseline, and hybridization with a transformer yields a marginal --- not transformational --- improvement. A null-to-small result is still a valid scientific finding, and it directly answers the thesis question.

\section{Medical Transfer Learning: Pretrained ResNet vs. From-Scratch CNN}
Counter-intuitively, the MedicalNet-\emph{pretrained} 3D ResNet ($69.8\%$) was clearly \emph{worse} than the from-scratch DenseNet ($81.0\%$), and far less stable (std $0.139$ vs $0.041$). Inspection of the per-fold curves indicates this is partly an \textbf{early-stopping artifact}: the pretrained ResNet has a late accuracy take-off, and with the shared patience budget three of the five folds are competitive while two stop before recovering, dragging the mean down and inflating the variance. This is an important, honest caveat --- it means the result is a property of the training schedule as much as the architecture, and a min-epoch guard before early stopping is a concrete fix flagged for future work rather than evidence that medical pretraining is useless.

\section{Why the From-Scratch ViT Collapses}
The pure 3D ViT reaches only $28.6\%$ on the three-class task --- essentially the $33\%$ chance level --- with almost no fold variance, i.e.\ it consistently fails to learn a useful decision boundary. This is the expected behaviour of a data-hungry, low-inductive-bias transformer trained from scratch on a few hundred subjects, and it is precisely why the literature relies on large-scale pretraining for ViTs~\cite{dosovitskiy2021image}. Reported as a negative result, it justifies the thesis design choice of pairing the transformer with a CNN (which supplies the missing inductive bias) rather than using a transformer alone.

\section{Answers to the Research Questions}
The evidence gathered above answers the two research questions posed in Chapter~\ref{cha:introduction} directly.

\noindent\textbf{RQ1 --- Does fusing local (CNN) and global (ViT) features improve accuracy?} \emph{Yes, but modestly.} The hybrid CNN--ViT is the single best binary model ($82.2\%$ / macro-F1 $0.802$), and it beats every standalone configuration tried. However, its advantage over a well-trained from-scratch DenseNet ($81.0\%$ / $0.788$) is within one standard deviation, so the honest statement is that fusion yields a small, consistent gain rather than a large one --- and only when a strong CNN is one of the branches (the Swin--ViT pairing, with no CNN, is far weaker). Hybridization helps; it is not a silver bullet.

\noindent\textbf{RQ2 --- Can 3D explainability give clinically meaningful explanations?} \emph{Yes, qualitatively.} Across Grad-CAM, Integrated Gradients, and attention rollout, and across CNN, Swin, and hybrid models, the importance consistently concentrates in medial-temporal and peri-ventricular regions that match the known AD signature (Figures~\ref{fig:xai_panel}--\ref{fig:xai_3class}). Because the volumes are not skull-stripped or atlas-registered, these are reported as coarse spatial regions rather than named ROIs, but the agreement across methods is strong evidence that the models key on biology, not artefacts.

\section{Threats to Validity}
Several factors bound the strength of these conclusions and are stated openly. \textbf{Sample size:} 395 subjects (only 80 MCI) is small, so the three-class estimates in particular carry wide fold-to-fold variance. \textbf{Single site:} using ADNI alone keeps the protocol clean but leaves cross-scanner, cross-population generalization untested. \textbf{Incomplete grid:} not every model$\times$task cell is filled (e.g., some standalone and hybrid three-class runs), so a few comparisons are indicative rather than exhaustive. \textbf{Training-schedule confound:} the pretrained-ResNet result is partly an early-stopping artifact rather than a pure architecture effect. \textbf{Explainability granularity:} without skull-stripping/registration, importance is spatial-region-level, not atlas-ROI-level. None of these overturn the headline findings, but each marks where stronger claims would require the additional work outlined in Chapter~\ref{cha:ConclusionAndFutureWork}.

\section{Summary}
On a leakage-free, subject-level ADNI benchmark, the proposed hybrid 3D CNN--ViT gives the best binary CN-vs-AD result ($82.2\%$, macro-F1 $0.80$), marginally beating a strong from-scratch DenseNet; the three-class ceiling is an honest $\sim\!57\%$, bottlenecked by MCI. Medical transfer learning (pretrained ResNet) underperformed, partly due to an early-stopping artifact, and a from-scratch ViT failed outright --- both reported as instructive negative results. Explainability maps localize to AD-relevant medial-temporal and ventricular regions, with the pipeline's limitations stated explicitly. Together these results answer the two research questions: hybridization helps, but modestly (RQ1); and 3D explainability yields anatomically plausible, if coarse, evidence (RQ2).
